# The website: templates showcase, hero motion and the phone header

**26 October, 16:38:** Monochrome joins the range as the sixteenth (`invoice-templates.md`), so the section's count, "See all sixteen" and the list below follow it. Where this spec says fifteen, read sixteen.

The owner's notes of 25 October 2026 (18:14 and 18:22) on the website after the new range of invoice templates.

## The templates section

The section says there are fifteen templates, but showed three, and only the one in front changed. It becomes a shelf.

- **Six, as designed.** A shelf of six templates, each made out for its own invented business as it was designed:
  Signature (Larkspur Analytics), Billboard (Overcast), Atelier in rose (Wilder & Rose), Marquee (Night Shift Sound),
  Trade (Kestrel Roofing) and Riso (Odd Hours Press). Each is named, with who it is for. One is in front; choosing
  another (pointer, tap or keyboard) brings it to the front.
- **Yours.** Typing a business name or choosing a colour redraws all six with the visitor's name and colour, keeping
  each template's own sample work. "Back to the examples" puts the six back as designed.
- **All fifteen.** "See all fifteen" opens the whole range, each named with who it is for.
- **Colours.** Eight chosen colours, one of them pale (a yellow), so the contrast rule and Marquee's second colour show.
  No colour picker on the website; the app has the full one.
- The section's heading keeps "Fifteen layouts. Your logo, your colour."

## The hero

One sequence when the page loads, played once, in reading order (the owner's notes of 25 October at 19:34 brought
back the two halves meeting):

1. The header comes down from the top; its logo, links and buttons follow it in, left to right.
2. The headline's two halves, cut along the crease, come in from opposite ends and meet: the near half from the left,
   the far half (grey) from the right.
3. The crease is drawn from its foot at the bottom left up to its top, starting as the halves close: slow to start,
   quick through the middle, slow at the top, its tip soft.
4. The two facts, the sentence and the buttons settle in, in that order.
5. The slabs rise, left to right, then their caption.

The whole sequence is settled inside two and a half seconds. With reduced motion asked for, the page is shown settled,
with nothing moving.

## The app below the hero

The three small screens on a tilted plane are replaced by the example studio's dashboard, large enough to read (the
same studio and the same six months as the hero's slabs), with a caption. Scrolling to it, it lands: it starts tipped
back and stands up to face the reader by the time it is in view. On phones it is cropped to its figures.

## Motion on scroll

Below the hero nothing plays on its own: it moves with the scroll and runs back when the page is scrolled back. Each
section's words rise into place as they arrive, in the order they are read, and each section has one moment of its
own: the dashboard standing up, the pillars' rules drawn one after another, the route through How it works, the shelf
dealt out from one stack, the phones set down, the invoice's lines and its slip, and the logo at the foot. Where the
browser cannot tie motion to the scroll, everything is in place.

## On phones

- The header holds the logo and the menu only; "Create free account" moves into the menu. The hero's own button stays.
- Type steps down on phones: body text 16 px and the hero's sentence 19 px. Labels (12 px capitals) and the hero's two
  facts (14 px) keep their sizes.

## Sample details in public

Every invented business shown in public (the showcase, the demo, the picker's previews) has details that look real but
can belong to no one: VAT numbers that fail the HMRC check digits (both the 97 and the 9755 schemes), bank account
numbers that fail the Vocalink modulus check for their sort code, company numbers far above any Companies House has issued (29 million or more; SC990000 or more in Scotland),
phone numbers from the ranges Ofcom keeps for drama, and domains that are not registered to the business named.
