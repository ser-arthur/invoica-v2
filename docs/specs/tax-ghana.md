# Ghana: VAT, levies and invoice requirements for Invoica

Research date: 18 October 2026. Every fact below carries its source link and the date shown on the source, or "no date shown" where the page prints none. "Checked" means read on 18 October 2026. Sources marked "secondary" are professional firm or news material, used only where no official text could be read, with their date.

## Summary

- Since 1 January 2026 the Value Added Tax Act, 2025 (Act 1151) governs VAT. A standard-rated supply carries VAT 15%, NHIL 2.5% and GETFund Levy 2.5%, all charged on the same base (the value of the supply excluding VAT and levies). The effective rate is 20%. The 1% COVID-19 Health Recovery Levy is abolished. NHIL and GETFund are now claimable as input tax.
- Before 1 January 2026 the levies (NHIL 2.5%, GETFund 2.5%, COVID-19 1%) were charged on the value, and VAT 15% was charged on the value plus the levies. The effective rate was 21.9%. Nothing is compound under the new rules.
- The VAT Flat Rate Scheme (3% on goods, 5% on immovable property) is abolished from 1 January 2026.
- Registration threshold for businesses dealing in goods: GH₵750,000 (up from GH₵200,000). For services, three secondary sources say there is no turnover threshold; GRA's own pages state only the goods threshold. This is an open point.
- VAT-registered businesses must issue tax invoices in the Commissioner-General's form. A registered person may use its own computer-generated invoices only with written authorisation, and from 2026 those must show separate lines for NHIL 2.5%, GETFund Levy 2.5% and VAT 15% plus a mandatory total of the three.
- E-VAT: since 2022 the VAT law has required taxable persons to issue tax invoices through a GRA-certified invoicing system integrated with GRA's system, applied in phases as the Commissioner-General directs. Onboarded businesses must issue invoices only from their certified system; each invoice carries a GRA QR code, signature, verification ID, encrypted data and time stamp. No official date by which every VAT-registered business must be onboarded was found.
- Withholding tax is deducted by the paying customer, so it is not charged on the supplier's invoice. Income tax withholding on services is 7.5%, on goods 3% and on works 5%; VAT withholding agents withhold 7% of the taxable value.
- Records: at least 6 years from the end of the VAT accounting period, kept within Ghana. VAT accounting software must be approved by the Commissioner-General.
- Currency: the cedi is the currency for all tax purposes. Foreign amounts convert at the Bank of Ghana interbank rate on the date. The Foreign Exchange Act, 2006 (Act 723) prohibits pricing and payment for goods and services in foreign currency in Ghana.

## 1. Taxes on a standard-rated supply

### Rates and base from 1 January 2026 (official)

GRA "Notice to All VAT Registered Taxpayers" (https://gra.gov.gh/news/portfolio/notice-to-all-vat-registered-taxpayers/ and https://gra.gov.gh/news/?p=6191; no date printed, image in the December 2025 upload folder; checked):

- "Following the passage of Value Added Tax Act, 2025 (Act 1151)"
- "The VAT rate remains at 15% while the NHIL and GETFund levies stay at 2.5% each, bringing the total effective VAT rate to 20%."
- "The COVID-19 Health Recovery Levy has been abolished."
- "The VAT Flat Rate Scheme (VFRS) has been abolished"
- "GETFund and NHIL levies will be treated as input tax deductions."
- "Effective Implementation of the VAT Act is January 1, 2026."

GRA VAT page (https://gra.gov.gh/domestic-tax/tax-types/vat/, no last-updated date shown, footer "Copyright 2025"; checked):

- "Value Added Tax (VAT) Rate = 15%", "National Health Insurance Levy (NHIL) = 2.5%", "Ghana Education Trust Fund Levy (GETFund) = 2.5%"
- On the COVID-19 levy: "The 1% levy has been abolished"
- On the base: "This implies that the VAT, the NHIL and the GETFund Levy are all charged on the same base or value." and "The value of the supply (goods and/or services) should be stated excluding values of levies and VAT."
- On input tax: "VAT Registered Taxpayers can now claim the NHIL and the GETFund charged on their purchases as input tax"
- Zero-rated and exempt: "Items that attract a zero (0%) rate of VAT also attract a zero rate of GETFund and the NHIL." "Supplies relieved from VAT are also relieved from NHIL and GETFund Levy." "Supplies exempted from VAT are also exempted from NHIL and GetFund Levy."
- Specific zero rates: locally manufactured sanitary towels at 0%, and locally manufactured textiles at 0% "until the 31stDecember 2028".

GRA's worked example on the same page, for a selling price of GH₵1,000:

| | From 1 January 2026 | Before 1 January 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| NHIL 2.5% | 25 (on 1,000) | 25 (on 1,000) |
| GETFund Levy 2.5% | 25 (on 1,000) | 25 (on 1,000) |
| COVID-19 Levy 1% | abolished | 10 (on 1,000) |
| VAT-able value | 1,000 | 1,060 (value plus levies) |
| VAT 15% | 150 (on 1,000) | 159 (on 1,060) |
| Total | 1,200 | 1,219 |
| Effective rate | 20% | 21.9% |

So the old structure was compound (VAT charged on value plus levies) and the new one is not.

## 2. VAT Flat Rate Scheme

- Abolished from 1 January 2026. GRA notice: "The VAT Flat Rate Scheme (VFRS) has been abolished with the introduction of a unified and more transparent VAT structure." GRA VAT page: "The 3% flat rate scheme on supply of goods and 5% flat rate scheme for supply of immovable properties have been removed in favour of a unified and transparent VAT structure."
- Transition (GRA VAT page): "Existing flat rate taxpayers who do not meet the registration threshold should remain registered until de-registered by the Commissioner-General." They may report to their tax office for "determination of registration status."
- Secondary (KPMG, February 2026): former 3% retailers and 5% property suppliers must charge the standard rate; former flat-rate traders may claim input tax on declared inventory, subject to conditions including a six-month limit.
- For Invoica: a flat-rate preset may only be used to reproduce invoices dated before 1 January 2026.

## 3. Registration thresholds

- Goods (official): "The threshold for VAT registration for businesses who deal in goods has been increased from GH₵200,000 to GH₵750,000." (GRA notice). The GRA VAT page says the same.
- Measurement period: secondary only. KPMG (February 2026): registration is required "once taxable supplies exceed GHS 750,000 over any twelve-month period", and administrative guidelines can require earlier registration, for example where supplies exceed GH₵62,500 in one month or less. EY (January 2026): "at least GHS750k during the year", pro-rated for monthly or quarterly periods.
- Services: sources disagree.
  - Secondary sources say there is no threshold. EY alert (12 January 2026): no turnover threshold for services; registration is required within 30 days of starting a taxable activity unless the Commissioner-General permits otherwise. KPMG (February 2026): service providers must register "irrespective of the value of the turnover", unless the Commissioner-General directs otherwise. Crowe Ghana (22 January 2026, https://www.crowe.com/gh/news/ghana-vat-reform-2026): "All service providers are required to register for VAT, irrespective of turnover."
  - GRA's notice and VAT page state only the goods threshold and say nothing about services. A GRA official on Joy FM, reported on 15 September 2026 (https://www.myjoyonline.com/businesses-above-gh%C2%A2750000-turnover-must-register-for-vat-gra/, news), gave a single rule: "Once you make taxable sales or taxable turnover exceeding GH¢750,000, you are required to register", without distinguishing services.
- GRA's Domestic Tax FAQ (https://gra.gov.gh/domestic-tax/domestic-tax-faq/, no date shown) still gives the old GHC 200,000 threshold and a three-month test (GHC 50,000). It is out of date.
- Consequence of not registering (news, same Joy FM report): "Even if you refuse to register, by law, we still consider you registered and liable to charge the tax". Secondary (EY, 12 January 2026): the non-registration penalty is a minimum of three times the tax payable.

## 4. What a VAT invoice must show

### VAT-registered business: tax invoice

The content list is in the Value Added Tax Regulations, 2016 (L.I. 2243), regulation 21(2) (https://gra.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/VALUE-ADDED-TAX-REGULATIONS-2016-L.I.-2243.pdf; made 30 June 2016, in force 3 August 2016; checked). These regulations were made under Act 870; their status under Act 1151 is not confirmed (see Open points). "A tax invoice shall contain the following:"

| Ref | Particular |
|---|---|
| (a) | "the name, address and Tax Identification Number of that taxable person;" |
| (b) | "the date and time of supply;" |
| (c) | "the number of the invoice taken from a consecutive series;" |
| (d) | "the name of the customer or business name and address and Tax Identification Number if a taxable person;" |
| (e) | "a description sufficient to identify the goods or services supplied including the quantity of the goods or the extent of the services supplied;" |
| (f) | "the type of transaction by reference to the following categories: (i) sale; (ii) hire purchase, hire, lease or rental; (iii) exchange; (iv) goods and services supplied from the taxable person's own supplies;" |
| (g) | "the tax-exclusive charge for each description of goods or services supplied;" |
| (h) | "the rate of the tax;" |
| (i) | "the total charge on the invoice, exclusive of the tax;" |
| (j) | "the rate of any discount;" |
| (k) | "the total tax charged; and" |
| (l) | "the total charge inclusive of the tax." |

Form of the invoice: regulation 21(3): "Unless a registered person is authorised by the Commissioner-General in writing to print that person's own invoice similar to the invoice prescribed by the Commissioner-General, the tax invoice issued by a registered person shall be the invoice printed by the Commissioner-General." Authorisation is time-limited and renewable (21(4)). Act 870 section 41(1), as substituted in 2022, required the tax invoice "in the form and with the details that are prescribed by the Commissioner-General" (https://gra.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/Value-Added-Tax-Amendment-Act-2022-Act-1082.pdf).

Changes from 1 January 2026 (GRA VAT page):

- "taxpayers authorized to use their own computer-generated invoices and sales receipts must modify them to provide separate lines for NHIL at 2.5%, GETFund levy at 2.5%, and a line for the VAT at 15%."
- "The Commissioner-General's invoice should contain fields for NHIL, GETFund levy, and VAT."
- "In completing the invoice, the field for total of NHIL, GETFund Levy and VAT is mandatory."
- "Where the amount is inclusive of NHIL, GETFund Levy & VAT, you must indicate the amount in the field labelled total tax inclusive value."
- "A modified VAT invoice isolating the levies from VAT will be issued to taxpayers."

E-VAT invoices: GRA's E-VAT guideline (GRA/AG/2024/005, issued 18 June 2024, https://gra.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/E-VAT-GUIDELINES_20240222.pdf) lists the fields in section 7.0(2). It adds to the regulation list the supplier's TIN "including Ghana Card PIN", the time of supply, the unit of measure, and the rate and amount of each levy. Section 7.0(3) adds the electronic security fields (see section 5). The guideline's levy list still includes the COVID-19 levy, which no longer applies.

Other rules:

- One invoice per supply: "a taxable person shall issue only one tax invoice or sales receipt for each taxable supply" (Act 870 section 41(7) as substituted by Act 1082).
- Sales receipts (L.I. 2243 regulation 22): the Commissioner-General may authorise sales receipts for low-value, high-volume cash supplies made through an approved electronic device; minimum content is supplier name and full address, TIN, receipt serial number, the gross amount including tax (or the amount and the tax), and the date. "A sales receipt does not qualify for input tax deduction." A registered purchaser can ask for a tax invoice referring to the receipt's serial number.
- Credit and debit notes: the E-VAT guideline (section 7.0(4)) requires the TIN and the original invoice number plus the E-VAT security fields. Act 870 section 45 required tax credit and debit notes to contain "the particulars specified in the Fourth Schedule"; that schedule was not read. The equivalent provisions in Act 1151 were not read.
- Secondary (EY, 12 January 2026): under Act 1151, issuing a fiscal receipt through a Fiscal Electronic Device under the Taxation (Use of Fiscal Electronic Device) Act, 2018 (Act 966) satisfies the tax invoice or sales receipt requirement, and purchasers must give their TIN to a vendor using such a device in order to claim input VAT.

### Business not registered for VAT

- No official invoice content rules for unregistered businesses were found in the tax laws read.
- An unregistered business must not show VAT or levies. Under Act 870, tax shown on an invoice was "recoverable as tax due from the person issuing the invoice or sales receipt" even where the issuer was not a taxable person (section 55), and a person not entitled to issue a tax invoice could not provide one (section 41). The equivalent sections of Act 1151 were not read.
- Records: every person must keep "underlying documents, however described in the nature of receipts, invoices, vouchers, contracts" (Revenue Administration Act, 2016 (Act 915), section 27(2); see section 7).
- Language: "English is the official language of this country and the Authority may refuse to recognise a communication or document that is not in the official language." (Act 915 section 20(1)).

## 5. E-VAT (certified invoicing)

### The legal duty

From the VAT (Amendment) Act, 2022 (Act 1082), assented 12 September 2022, which substituted section 41 of Act 870 (https://gra.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/Value-Added-Tax-Amendment-Act-2022-Act-1082.pdf, checked):

- Section 41(2): "A taxable person shall (a) issue a tax invoice through a Certified Invoicing System; and (b) ensure that the Certified Invoicing System of the taxable person is integrated into the invoicing system of the Commissioner-General."
- Section 41(3): the Commissioner-General "may access the Certified Invoicing System of a taxable person to ensure compliance".
- Section 41(8): a taxable customer who has not received a tax invoice may, "within forty-eight hours after the date of the supply, obtain a copy of the invoice from the Certified Invoicing System of the taxable person."
- Section 41(9): if the system goes offline, the taxable person must "within twenty-four hours" inform the Commissioner-General and restore it.
- Section 65 definitions: "Certified Invoicing System" means "an electronic invoicing system certified by the CommissionerGeneral in accordance with this Act"; "tax invoice" "includes an electronic tax invoice issued through a Certified Invoicing System".
- Penalty, section 41(11): issuing a false tax invoice, failing to issue a tax invoice, failing "to issue a tax invoice through a Certified Invoicing System contrary to subsection (2)", tampering with the system, or failing to integrate or reconnect it. Act 1082 set the penalty at "not more than five hundred currency points or three times the amount of tax involved, whichever is higher". The VAT (Amendment) (No. 2) Act, 2022 (Act 1087), assented 2 December 2022, substituted subsection (11) with a penalty of not more than fifty thousand currency points or three times the tax involved, whichever is higher (https://gra.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/VAT-AMENDMENT-2022.pdf; the GRA copy has text recognition errors, so the figure should be checked by eye).
- Timing, section 41(12) as substituted by Act 1087: "Except as otherwise directed by the Commissioner-General, a taxable person shall, upon the coming into force of this Act, comply with the provisions of this section."
- Under Act 1151: secondary sources (EY, January 2026) place tax invoices in section 43 and the penalty for failing to issue a tax invoice in section 66. Whether section 43 keeps the Certified Invoicing System duty in the same words is not confirmed.

### Who must use it and when

- GRA's E-VAT guideline (GRA/AG/2024/005, 18 June 2024) section 6.0 covers "all VAT registered taxpayers", whether they invoice electronically or by hand, and non-registered or one-off suppliers who need VAT invoices. Section 16.0: implementation is "carried out in phases as determined by the Commissioner-General". Section 10.0(a): a taxpayer may be selected, may request onboarding, or may be onboarded when applying for VAT registration.
- GRA's E-VAT page (https://gra.gov.gh/e-services/e-vat/, no date shown; it states that Act 1151 "took effect on 1st January 2026") describes E-VAT as "an electronic system for issuing VAT receipts" and "not a new tax". It gives no deadline. To request E-VAT, a business writes to its Taxpayer Service Centre.
- Progress (news, Daily Graphic, 2 June 2025, https://www.graphic.com.gh/news/general-news/ghana-news-e-vat-system-gra-signs-on-3-000-businesses-5-000-more-prepare-to-onboard.html): over 3,000 businesses onboarded since 2022; GRA was reviewing the whole system to capture all VAT-registered taxpayers. Secondary (VATCalc, 20 May 2025, updated 2 June 2025): phases from 2022, with a target of 40,000 taxpayers by Q4 2025 given only in a subheading.
- Fiscal Electronic Devices: secondary (KPMG TaxNewsFlash, 13 November 2025, https://kpmg.com/us/en/taxnewsflash/news/2025/11/ghana-mandatory-use-fiscal-electronic-devices-2026.html): GRA said in August 2025 it would fully implement the Taxation (Use of Fiscal Electronic Device) Act 2018 by early 2026 for "specified taxable persons". News (Citi Newsroom, 28 July 2026, https://www.citinewsroom.com/2026/07/parliament-approves-gra-deal-to-roll-out-fiscal-electronic-device-policy/): Parliament approved a contract to deploy 40,000 fiscal devices.
- No official date by which all VAT-registered businesses must issue invoices through a certified system was found.

### What an invoice produced outside a certified system means

- For a registered business that has been onboarded (or directed to comply): the law requires its tax invoices to be issued through its certified system, and failing to do so attracts the section 41(11) penalty. The E-VAT guideline (section 18.0) says GRA officers check that invoices are issued "exclusively from the taxpayers' Certified Invoicing System". Section 7.0(5): "A tax invoice shall not be issued in any circumstance other than that specified in Regulation 21(1)."
- For a registered business not yet onboarded: its tax invoice must be the Commissioner-General's printed invoice, or its own computer-generated invoice under written authorisation (L.I. 2243 regulation 21(3); GRA VAT page, 2026).
- Input tax for the customer: under Act 870 section 48(1)(a)(ii), input tax on local purchases required possession of "a tax invoice issued under this Act". A sales receipt never qualifies (L.I. 2243 regulation 22(6)).
- Inference for Invoica, not stated by any source: a PDF generated by Invoica is a valid Ghanaian tax invoice only if the business holds Commissioner-General authorisation for its own computer-generated invoices and is not yet onboarded to E-VAT, or if Invoica is integrated as the business's certified system. Otherwise the Invoica document can serve as a commercial invoice or statement, and the tax invoice must come from GRA's system.

### What an integration involves

From the E-VAT guideline (GRA/AG/2024/005) and GRA's E-VAT page:

- Two routes (guideline section 9.0; E-VAT page): integrate the taxpayer's own certified ERP or POS with GRA's invoicing system, or use GRA's free invoicing software (desktop for Windows, Android mobile, browser-based online version, and a portal for one-off transactions). GRA staff install either option.
- Onboarding steps (section 10.0): selection or request, a GRA Relationship Manager, an onboarding form, choice of route, then for integration "API documentation, system integration, testing, GRA sign-off, go-live". Estimated time: about 1 month for API integration and about 2 weeks for the free software.
- Components (section 5.0): the Certified Invoicing Management System; a Virtual Sales Data Controller, "a software module communicating with both the CIMS and the Authority for processing receipts"; an online sales data controller that validates and authenticates invoices.
- Required on each certified invoice (section 7.0(3)): QR code, invoice signature, verification engine ID, encrypted data and time stamp. GRA's E-VAT page lists the receipt features as the "Commissioner-General's Signature", a QR code for verification, an unalterable receipt signature, an exact date and time, a serial number, a receipt number and a machine registration code. Buyers are told to "Scan QR Code on electronic invoices to verify" (GRA VAT page).
- Operation: the platform requests a Signature Key from GRA; receipts can be generated "online or offline for up to 24 hours" (E-VAT page). If the system is down, notify GRA at once (support@evatgra.zendesk.com in the guideline), and upload offline transactions on restoration; give 24 hours' notice of upgrades (section 11.0). GRA must have automated access to system logs (section 18.0).
- Cancellations after a return is filed need a tax debit note (section 12.1). Only non-discriminatory discounts are recognised, and tax is charged on the discounted value (section 12.3).
- Whether GRA certifies a multi-tenant software product once for all its users, or only each taxpayer's integration separately, is not stated. The API specification is issued to onboarded taxpayers and was not available publicly.

## 6. Withholding taxes on invoices to companies

Income tax withholding, resident payees (GRA Withholding Tax page, https://gra.gov.gh/domestic-tax/tax-types/withholding-tax/, no date shown, footer "Copyright 2025"; checked):

| Payment | Rate |
|---|---|
| Supply of services by an entity (over GH¢2,000 per annum) | 7.5% |
| Supply of general services by an individual | 7.5% |
| Supply of goods (over GH¢2,000 per annum) | 3% |
| Supply of works (over GH¢2,000 per annum) | 5% |
| Fees or allowances to resident individual directors, managers, board members and trustees | 20% |
| Commission to individual insurance, sales, canvassing and lotto agents | 10% |
| Rent on non-residential property | 15% |
| Rent on residential property | 8% |

Non-resident payees: 20% on goods, works or any services and on management and technical service fees (same page).

PwC Worldwide Tax Summaries (secondary, last reviewed 11 March 2026, https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/ghana/corporate/withholding-taxes) gives the same resident rates (3%, 5%, 7.5%, 7.5%).

How it works (GRA Practice Note DT/2016/001, "Withholding of Tax", issued 6 October 2016, https://gra.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/Practice-Note-on-Withholding-of-Tax.pdf):

- "A resident person other than an individual, shall withhold tax on the gross amount of a payment" (section 4.2.2). Payments "less than or equal to GHS2,000.00 shall not attract withholding tax" (section 4.6).
- The base "shall be exclusive of any Value Added Tax" (section 4).
- The withholding agent "shall prepare and serve on a withholdee a Withholding Tax Certificate" (section 7), and the supplier gets a tax credit equal to the tax withheld (section 8).

VAT withholding (GRA VAT Withholding page, https://gra.gov.gh/domestic-tax/tax-types/vat-withholding/, no date shown, cites Act 870): agents appointed by the Commissioner-General "withhold from payments for standard rated supplies 7% of the taxable output value for VAT purposes", and must "issue a Withholding VAT Credit Certificate at the time of payment". The supplier still files its own VAT return. The page's description of the balance paid to the supplier reflects the pre-2026 levy structure. PwC (secondary, 11 March 2026) still gives 7%. Secondary (EY, January 2026): under Act 1151 the Commissioner-General may exempt a compliant taxpayer from VAT withholding in writing.

Does the supplier show withholding on the invoice? No source requires it. The customer deducts it at payment and gives the supplier a certificate. The invoice shows the full amount due. Invoica may offer an optional memo line ("Withholding tax to be deducted by customer: 7.5% of the value excluding VAT"), and should let a payment be marked as settled by cash plus a withholding certificate.

## 7. Record keeping

- Revenue Administration Act, 2016 (Act 915), section 27 (https://gra.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/Revenue-Administration-Act-2016-.pdf, checked):
  - 27(1): "A person shall maintain, within the country, necessary records".
  - 27(2): records include "receipts, invoices, vouchers, contracts or in the case of electronic records, any medium by which the information can be extracted."
  - 27(3): documents must be kept "for a period of at least six years from the relevant date", or longer while an objection, appeal, application, refund claim or investigation is open.
  - 27(5): for VAT the "relevant date" is "the end of the accounting period or periods for which the document is relevant"; for income tax, the end of the year of assessment.
- VAT Regulations, 2016 (L.I. 2243), regulation 31:
  - 31(1)(c) and (f): keep "copies of all tax invoices and sales receipts issued" and "all debit and credit notes".
  - 31(2): "The accounting software used by a registered person shall be one that is approved by the Commissioner-General."
  - 31(3): electronic records need the Commissioner-General's permission, with unrestricted access during business hours, print-outs on request, and ICT support for audits.
  - 31(4): keep records "for a minimum period of six years" and destroy them afterwards "only with the written permission of the Commissioner-General."
- The E-VAT guideline (section 13.0) repeats the six-year minimum.

## 8. Currency

- Revenue Administration Act, 2016 (Act 915), section 21(1): "The Cedi is the official currency for purposes of the tax laws and, subject to any provision in a tax law to the contrary, every amount taken into account under a tax law is to be denominated in or converted into Cedis." Section 21(2): conversion "shall be at the Bank of Ghana interbank exchange rate applying on the date the amount is to be taken into account". Section 21(3): the Commissioner-General may, on written application, allow a foreign-currency basis for records and accounts.
- Bank of Ghana notice BG/GOV/SEC/2019/07 (https://www.bog.gov.gh/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Prohibition-of-Pricing-Payment-for-Goods-and-Services-in-Foreign-Currency.pdf; no date in the text, July 2019 upload folder): the Foreign Exchange Act, 2006 (Act 723) "prohibits the pricing, advertising and receipt or payment for goods and services in foreign currency"; breach is "punishable by summary conviction" with "a fine of up to seven hundred penalty units" or "a prison term of not more than eighteen months, or both". The notice names no section of Act 723 and lists no exceptions.
- For Invoica: a Ghana-based business invoicing Ghanaian customers for supplies in Ghana should invoice in cedis (GHS). Tax amounts on any Ghanaian tax invoice must be in cedis or converted at the Bank of Ghana interbank rate for the date. Whether exports, or invoices from a business outside Ghana to a Ghanaian customer, fall under the prohibition is not confirmed.

## Presets for Invoica (Ghana)

All three current taxes are charged on the same base (the line's value excluding VAT and levies). None is compound. They apply together to every standard-rated supply and are shown as three separate lines plus a total.

Earlier VAT rates (before the 15% rate) and the 5% flat rate on immovable property are left out; their dates were not confirmed.

Tax-inclusive prices: under the 2026 structure, value = inclusive price ÷ 1.20, and each tax is its rate times that value. GRA requires the inclusive amount to be shown in the field "total tax inclusive value".

Rounding: no Ghanaian rounding rule was found. Proposed rule (a design choice): compute NHIL, GETFund Levy and VAT separately on the invoice's standard-rated value subtotal, as in GRA's worked example, and round each to two decimal places with half up. Store the unrounded figures.

Withholding taxes are not tax presets. Offer them as an optional memo and as a payment-settlement type.

## Invoice fields the Ghana template must show

### A. Business not registered for VAT (sole trader or company)

- Business name, address and contact details
- Invoice number and date
- Customer name and address
- Description, quantity and price of each item
- Total amount in cedis
- No VAT, NHIL or GETFund lines
- English language
- Recommended (not confirmed as required): the supplier's TIN or Ghana Card PIN, and for companies the registered name and company number

### B. VAT-registered business without E-VAT, holding authorisation for its own computer-generated invoices

From L.I. 2243 regulation 21(2) and the 2026 GRA changes:

- Supplier's name, address and TIN
- Date and time of supply
- Invoice number from a consecutive series
- Customer's name or business name and address, and the customer's TIN if a taxable person
- Description with quantity of goods or extent of services
- Transaction type: sale; hire purchase, hire, lease or rental; exchange; or own supplies
- Tax-exclusive charge for each line
- Rate of tax
- Total charge exclusive of tax
- Rate of any discount
- Separate lines for NHIL at 2.5%, GETFund Levy at 2.5% and VAT at 15%
- Mandatory total of NHIL, GETFund Levy and VAT
- Total charge inclusive of tax ("total tax inclusive value")
- Zero-rated and exempt lines marked
- Amounts in cedis

Without written authorisation, the business must use the Commissioner-General's printed invoice, and Invoica cannot produce the tax invoice.

### C. VAT-registered business onboarded to E-VAT

All of B, plus the supplier's TIN including Ghana Card PIN, the unit of measure, and the GRA-issued QR code, invoice signature, verification engine ID, encrypted data and time stamp. The invoice must be issued through the certified system, so Invoica could only produce it as an integrated system with GRA sign-off.

### D. Credit and debit notes (registered business)

The supplier's TIN, the original invoice number, and, on E-VAT, the QR code, signature, verification engine ID, encrypted data and time stamp. The full statutory list under Act 1151 was not read.

## Open points

1. The text of the Value Added Tax Act, 2025 (Act 1151) and GRA's "VAT Guidelines for VAT Act 1151" could not be read (scanned PDFs). Section numbers cited for Act 1151 are from EY and are secondary. The assent date is not confirmed.
2. Registration for service providers: EY, KPMG and Crowe say there is no turnover threshold; GRA's notice and VAT page mention only the goods threshold; a GRA official in September 2026 gave a single GH₵750,000 rule. Needs GRA's written confirmation or the Act text.
3. The measurement period for the GH₵750,000 threshold (any 12 months, calendar year, or a one-month GH₵62,500 test) is secondary only.
4. Whether L.I. 2243 (VAT Regulations, 2016), made under Act 870, still applies under Act 1151, including the invoice content list (regulation 21), the Commissioner-General's printed invoice rule (21(3)) and approved accounting software (31(2)). GRA's 2026 VAT page assumes the authorisation regime continues.
5. Whether Act 1151 keeps the Certified Invoicing System duty and penalty of Act 870 section 41 (as amended by Acts 1082 and 1087) in the same terms; the penalty figure in Act 1087 (fifty thousand currency points) comes from a copy with text recognition errors.
6. No official date by which every VAT-registered business must issue invoices through E-VAT. No public API specification; whether GRA certifies a software product for all its users is unknown. Ask GRA's E-VAT team before building an integration.
7. Credit and debit note content under Act 1151 (the Fourth Schedule equivalent) was not read.
8. VAT withholding: the 7% rate is confirmed by GRA's page (which cites Act 870) and by PwC (March 2026); whether Act 1151 changed the rate or base is not confirmed.
9. Whether the Income Tax (Amendment) Act, 2026 (Act 1178), effective 1 September 2026, changed any withholding rate. GRA's summary of that Act covers personal rates and the modified taxation scheme and mentions no withholding change; its full text was not read.
10. No Ghanaian rule on rounding VAT and levies was found. Two decimal places with half up is a design choice.
11. Section 27(1) of Act 915 requires records to be maintained "within the country". Whether records held by a cloud service outside Ghana satisfy this, and whether L.I. 2243 regulation 31(3) permission for electronic records is needed, is not confirmed.
12. Whether Act 723's ban on foreign-currency pricing covers exports or invoices from a supplier outside Ghana to a Ghanaian customer, and which exceptions apply, is not confirmed. The section number of Act 723 is not confirmed.
13. Mandatory content for invoices of unregistered businesses (for example TIN or company number disclosure under the TIN Act or the Companies Act, 2019) was not researched.
14. How a UK Invoica user's invoice to a Ghanaian business customer is treated (the customer accounting for VAT on imported services) was not checked against Act 1151. GRA's FAQ text on imported services predates the Act.
15. Earlier Ghanaian VAT and levy rates and their dates (before the 15% rate and before the COVID-19 levy) were not confirmed, so no legacy presets are proposed for them.
